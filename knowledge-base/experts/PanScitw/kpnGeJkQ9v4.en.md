At that time, transistors were still primarily constructed using a mesa structure These transistors were usually made of germanium stacking semiconductors layers with impurities one on top of the other Of course, this wasn’t just a simple process of stacking materials like kids building blocks Every piece got a specific shape and every layer of the transistor required additional processing to be precisely shaped. To achieve this, they used wax to protect the material exposing only the areas that needed to be etched away through chemical etching This semiconductor processing method was conceived by Harry Sello in Shockley’s laboratory The reason to use wax was that hydrofluoric acid which was used for etching corrodes both silicon and germanium but wax is resistant to it When hydrofluoric acid was applied, the areas not covered by wax were gradually etched into the desired shape while the wax-covered areas remained intact. It’s somewhat similar to how children make sand paintings. The entire drawing board is initially covered with stickers and only when the stickers are removed to expose the adhesive can it be coated with colorful sand Sello’s method was called wax masking It was indeed a simple and effective method. However, as transistors became smaller in size and more intricate in design, problems began to emerge Researchers discovered that it’s hard to draw precise and intricate graphs with wax Moreover,the fluidity of wax caused distortions at the edge of the graphs The smaller the graph, the greater the margin of error Jay Lathrop and Jim Null of Diamond Labs were stuck in their efforts How could they solve this problem? Surprisingly, the answer they found at last came from a company that produced celluloid Eastman Kodak Before charge-coupled devices (CCDs) and digital cameras were invented film was the primary way to take photos Films will be coated with an emulsion of “silver halide” crystals silver halides include compounds like silver bromide, silver iodide and silver chloride-- all of which are highly sensitive to light When a photo is taken, the shutter opens and closes rapidly, exposing the film for only a brief moment Lights passing through the lens induces a redox reaction in the silver ions causing them to transform from transparent ions into opaque silver atoms However, these silver atoms are extremely small and invisible to the naked eye which is why they are referred to as a “latent image.” During the film development process these silver atoms interact with a developer solution catalyzing the reduction of nearby silver ions back into metallic silver This process darkens the emulsion forming a visible black-and-white image The unexposed areas, which lack a latent image, remain transparent Lastly the fixer dissolves the unreacted transparent silver halide without redox reaction leaving behind a negative image on the film After that a negative-to-positive reversal process occurs but let’s skip that for now The point here is that to make better photographs such as those with higher resolution or full-color images that surpass black-and-white ones Kodak invested heavily in developing various photosensitive materials not just silver halides The material Lathrop chose for use in transistor fabrication was a product known as “photoresist” Photoresist also undergoes chemical changes when exposed to ultraviolet lights But the point isn’t to get black but to alter its dissolution rate The exposed areas harden while the unexposed parts remain unchanged and can be dissolved by a developer leaving behind the desired graphs So, how can light be controlled to selectively expose specific areas of the photoresist? They found inspiration in everyday microscopes The purpose of a microscope is to magnify light reflected from objects using lenses But what if we reversed the microscope? Could we then focus light into a tiny area using lenses? By placing light-blocking masks on the microscope and then shining light through it we could project a miniature version of the same graphs onto a tiny photoresist layer The mask that controls how light passes through is now commonly known as a “reticle.” Simply put this method replaces the material-protecting wax with photoresist And the way to etch photoresist is using reticles and a reversed microscope lens to ”expose” the photoresist Allowing the inherent properties of the photoresist to achieve the effect of etching Lathrop named this light-printing method photolithography a combination of the words : “photo” and “lithography” Photolithography technology quickly revolutionized the semiconductor industry This process not only improved the precision of transistor manufacturing but the introduction of reticles enabled the high-quality,,large-scale production of transistors Most importantly, photolithography paved the way for future chip miniaturization In 1957 Lathrop and Null
