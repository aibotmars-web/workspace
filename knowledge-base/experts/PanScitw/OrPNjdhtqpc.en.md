The small water valve in a transistor is called a “gate”, similar to a water gate The inlet and outlet pipes are called the “source” and “drain” The source and drain are like two reservoirs at the upstream and downstream ends of a river To move water from the source to the drain the water valve controlled by the gate must be opened Among them in the most popular transistor structure used in logic chips the gate has a sandwich-like structure composed of metal, an insulating oxide layer and semiconductor material Transistors of this type are commonly known as MOSFETs A single transistor structure But TSMC can now fit 200 billion tiny transistors like this in a single chip This number is expected to surpass a trillion soon How? Obviously the only way is to further shrink transistors But as transistors shrink the first challenge they face is water valve leakage Usually the water valves in transistors are designed to shut tightly to resist horizontal hydraulic pressure namely the vertical electric field applied to the gate must be far stronger than horizontal field between the source and drain If a closed water valve fails to completely stop current flow water spills over leading to increased power consumption Your smartphone would always need  to be connected to a power bank or worse, the water valve could fail entirely causing a total breakdown The entire transistor becomes invalid As transistors shrink the driving voltage used in circuits remained at about 0.7 volts It means the valve is getting smaller while the hydraulic pressure stays the same The valve ends up unable to stop leakage caused by overflow current And it’s not just leakage-- When the source and drain get closer various short-channel effects happen just like elderly folks develop more chronic diseases over time including velocity saturation parasitic BJT hot carrier effects and threshold voltage roll-off, among others. Engineers struggled to keep up just like doctors watching red figures on a health report For the miniaturization limit of transistors have we found a solution? To address the chronic disease called short channel effects academia and industry have worked together proposing various methods to keep Moore’s Law going For example, semiconductor materials are modified by doping silicon with other elements To suppress leakage precisely materials experts experimented with mixing different elements at specific locations within transistors much like knitting a wool blanket placing fibers of different colors in designated areas The technique is called ion implantation Who would have thought that semiconductors could draw inspiration from handicraft? Another approach was to thin the insulation oxide layer in transistors making it easier for the vertical electric field to pass through the gate channel thereby enhancing control over the water valve
