First, some context: A laser diode emits a continuous light wave when biased with a constant current above its threshold An external modulator is an optical modulator that comes after a laser diode and is controlled by an electrical signal It in turn controls the light intensity in a waveguide, which means it controls the “on” and “off” states of light This is the optical modulator currently used in photonic ICs and they basically work like this In intrinsic silicon semiconductors —pure silicon— a change in the concentrations of electrons and holes cause slight variations in the refractive index If a piece of pure silicon is sandwiched between a p-type and an n-type semiconductor, applying a voltage across the ends can alter the carrier concentration in the middle, thereby changing its refractive index But how does that help switch a laser beam “on” and “off” within a waveguide? That's where the microring resonator comes in —also known as the “ring resonator” Although such a ring-shaped waveguide isn't physically connected to a straight waveguide, its ability to resonate with the light determines whether the light can pass through the straight waveguide You see, A ring resonator has a certain resonance wavelength When it is tuned to match the wavelength of the light in a straight waveguide, a traveling wave resonance forms in the ring, thereby transferring energy from the straight waveguide to the ring through coupling As a result no light remains in the straight waveguide This corresponds to an “off” state When the ring's wavelength doesn't match the light's, no resonance occurs, and the energy remains in the straight waveguide This corresponds to an “on” state Now, remember that the carrier concentration in the ring waveguide changes its refractive index, thereby tuning its resonance wavelength So there's no need to change the laser's wavelength Instead, by putting a p-type and an n-type semiconductor on each side of a ring waveguide, we can tweak its resonance wavelength and modulate continuous light The ring resonator enables high-frequency modulation in a space of tens of microns In 2010 Intel used this design along with the hybrid silicon laser to launch a transceiver module with a transmission speed of 50 gigabits —or 50 billion bits—per second It marked the commercial debut of silicon photonics, but that was more than a decade ago So how has silicon photonics developed since 2010?
